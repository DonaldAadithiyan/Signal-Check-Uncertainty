\title{Endogenous Predictive-Difficulty Representations in Recurrent World Models}

\author{\authorname{Anonymous Author(s)}
\affiliation{Affiliation withheld for double-blind review}
\email{email withheld for double-blind review}
}

\keywords{World Models, Recurrent State-Space Models, Model-Based Reasoning, Mechanistic Interpretability, Causal Intervention.}

\abstract{Learned recurrent world models maintain compact states that summarise what is needed for future prediction. We ask whether these states also encode the evolving difficulty of their own prediction process, without any supervision for such a variable. In a Mini-DreamerV3 world model trained on three dm-control tasks, we find that the deterministic recurrent state carries a readout that tracks a discounted history of high-surprise steps, including when the current step is excluded from that history. This history predicts future divergence between imagined and real trajectories beyond KL divergence, reconstruction error, and exponentially smoothed versions of both, and the result is robust to how the history is defined. Causal intervention then separates two properties that are easily conflated: steering the hidden state along the readout direction decisively changes the model's internal difficulty readout, including ten recurrent steps later, while leaving the external quality of its imagined predictions at the level of random directions. Recurrent world models thus develop internal monitors of predictive difficulty that are informative about, but causally separable from, the quality of their own predictions.}

\section{\uppercase{Introduction}}
\label{sec:intro}

Learned recurrent world models maintain compact internal states that must summarise the information relevant for future prediction \cite{ha2018worldmodels,hafner2019planet,hafner2020dreamer}. These states let an agent predict and imagine the consequences of its actions without acting in the environment. An open question is whether the same states also encode information about the evolving \emph{difficulty} of their own prediction process.

The question matters because the obvious difficulty signals are instantaneous. A world model's KL divergence between posterior and prior, or its reconstruction error, reports how surprising the current step is. A recurrent state, by contrast, is a running summary of the past, and could in principle carry a \emph{history} of predictive difficulty: not only whether the present is surprising, but whether predictions have recently and repeatedly been hard. Whether such a quantity emerges, what it means, and how it is used are basic questions about what world models represent.

We therefore ask: \textbf{does a recurrent world model spontaneously encode accumulated predictive difficulty in its hidden state, without explicit supervision for such a variable?}

Answering this requires more than fitting a probe. A readout that predicts current KL is partly circular, since KL is computed from the same state, and a history statistic that includes the current step partly measures the probe's own label. A readout that only tracks smoothed recent error would add nothing a running average could not provide. And a readout that can be moved by intervention is not thereby shown to control the capability it appears to report \cite{elazar2021amnesic,joshi2026causal}. We address each of these points on a Mini-DreamerV3 recurrent state-space model (RSSM) \cite{hafner2025dreamerv3} trained on three dm-control tasks \cite{tassa2018dmcontrol}. Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Emergent representation.} A linear readout of the hidden state tracks a discounted history of high-KL steps, including a history that excludes the current step, beyond what current KL explains (Section~\ref{sec:emergence}).
\item \textbf{External meaning.} This history predicts future divergence between imagined and real trajectories, a quantity defined entirely outside its discovery signal, beyond KL, reconstruction error and their exponential smoothings, across definitions of the history (Section~\ref{sec:external}).
\item \textbf{Causal dissociation.} Steering the readout direction moves the model's internal difficulty readout far beyond random directions, immediately and ten recurrent steps later, without changing the external quality of its imagined predictions (Section~\ref{sec:causal}).
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec:interpretation} interprets these findings, including a filtering-theoretic account of when accumulated predictive difficulty should carry information.

\section{\uppercase{Related Work}}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Reliability in World Models}

Model-based agents manage model error by estimating it: probabilistic ensembles \cite{chua2018pets}, short model rollouts justified by error bounds \cite{janner2019mbpo}, disagreement-driven exploration \cite{sekar2020plan2explore}, and uncertainty-penalised rewards in offline settings \cite{yu2020mopo}. More general tools include deep ensembles \cite{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles}, the aleatoric/epistemic decomposition \cite{kendall2017uncertainties}, prediction-error signals \cite{pathak2017curiosity}, and out-of-distribution scores \cite{hendrycks2017baseline}. In each case a reliability quantity is \emph{constructed} by the designer. We investigate one that \emph{emerges} inside the recurrent predictive state without explicit supervision, and compare it against the cheap constructed signals a designer would reach for first.

\subsection{Representations in Learned Models}

Recurrent world models carry a deterministic belief state alongside a stochastic latent \cite{hafner2019planet,hafner2021dreamerv2}. Sequence models trained only on prediction develop structured internal representations of their environment \cite{li2023othello}, and language models carry readable information about whether their own answers are likely correct \cite{kadavath2022know}. We study not what the recurrent state represents about the task in general, but whether it develops a representation of its \emph{own} predictive difficulty, validated against an external ground truth.

\subsection{Mechanistic Interpretability}

Linear probes \cite{alain2016probes} establish that information can be read from a representation, not that the model uses it \cite{hewitt2019control,belinkov2022probing,elazar2021amnesic}. Interventional methods such as activation patching, editing and steering \cite{meng2022rome,geiger2021abstractions,turner2023actadd,zou2023repe} provide stronger evidence, and interpretability claims should state which rung of this causal ladder they reach \cite{joshi2026causal}. We use steering along a probe direction, scored against random-direction nulls, and measure both an internal and an external outcome of the same intervention.

\subsection{World Model}

We use Mini-DreamerV3 in its XS configuration \cite{hafner2025dreamerv3}: a 256-dimensional GRU \cite{cho2014gru} deterministic state $h_t$ and a $32{\times}32$ categorical stochastic latent $z_t$. The deterministic state is updated as
\begin{equation}
h_t = \mathrm{GRU}\big([z_{t-1}, a_{t-1}],\, h_{t-1}\big),
\label{eq:gru}
\end{equation}
and every analysis logs states with exactly this pairing: the observation $o_t$ enters the posterior together with the action $a_{t-1}$ that produced it, as in training. The model's per-step KL divergence $\KL_t$ between posterior and prior over $z_t$, and its observation reconstruction error $\Rec_t$, are its instantaneous surprise signals. For each task we train a primary model and additional independently initialised replicates (five models in total for cartpole, four each for reacher and pendulum), each for 100{,}000 environment steps on data collected by a uniform-random policy. All analyses query frozen models; which models enter each analysis is stated with its results.

\subsection{Data Splits}

All analyses run on fresh random-policy episodes of 500 steps, split by episode with fixed seeds: 100 \emph{probe-fit} episodes (probe, principal components), 50 \emph{calibration} episodes (every threshold, discount and smoothing constant), and 100 \emph{evaluation} episodes, on which every reported number is computed. The first 12 steps of each episode are excluded as burn-in. No split is made at the level of individual steps.

\subsection{Predictive-Difficulty History}

We summarise the recent history of predictive difficulty as a discounted count of high-KL steps,
\begin{equation}
C_t = \sum_{i \ge 0} \gamma^{i}\, \mathbb{1}\!\left[\KL_{t-i} > \tau\right],
\qquad
\Cp = \sum_{i \ge 1} \gamma^{i}\, \mathbb{1}\!\left[\KL_{t-i} > \tau\right],
\label{eq:ct}
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is the median of $\KL$ on the calibration episodes and the sums reset at episode boundaries. The $i{=}0$ term of $C_t$ is the label of the probe introduced in Section~\ref{sec:emergence}; $\Cp$ excludes it and is our primary statistic wherever the current step would make a comparison circular. For each statistic, $\gamma$ is chosen from $\{0.5, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95\}$ as the value whose statistic best fits the probe readout on the calibration episodes ($C_t$: cartpole \res{p1.cartpole.primary.variants.C.gamma}{.2f}, reacher \res{p1.reacher.primary.variants.C.gamma}{.2f}, pendulum \res{p1.pendulum.primary.variants.C.gamma}{.2f}). Section~\ref{sec:robust} reports every alternative definition we considered.

\subsection{External Target}

The external target is defined without reference to KL or to any probe. From the state at time $t$ the model imagines $K$ steps open-loop with the prior, feeding the real actions $a_t, \dots, a_{t+K-1}$ of the continuation; each imagined state is decoded and compared with the real observation:
\begin{equation}
\Est_{t,K} = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} \big\| \hat{o}_{t+k} - o_{t+k} \big\|_2,
\label{eq:estate}
\end{equation}
with $K \in \{1,5,10,20\}$. $\Est$ is computed at every site of the 100 evaluation episodes (\res{p2.cartpole.n_sites}{d}, \res{p2.reacher.n_sites}{d} and \res{p2.pendulum.n_sites}{d} sites).

\subsection{Statistical Protocol}

Every causal effect is scored as a $z$ value against the same manipulation applied along 50 random unit directions in $h$-space with the same step size; on each task's primary model we repeat this against 500 random directions and against 50 random directions confined to the orthogonal complement of the top-50 principal subspace. All intervals are 95\% bootstrap intervals from 1{,}000 resamples with episodes as the resampling unit; multi-model results report mean $\pm$ standard deviation over independently trained world models. Every stochastic step (environment, actions, and every latent sample) draws from an explicitly seeded generator, so each result is reproduced exactly on re-execution. Following \cite{joshi2026causal}, we use ``encodes'' and ``controls'' only for interventional results.

\subsection{Hidden-State Probe}

A logistic-regression probe on $h_t$, fitted on the probe-fit episodes, predicts whether $\KL_t$ exceeds $\tau$. Its weight vector $v$ defines the model's \emph{internal predictive-difficulty readout}. On cartpole the probe separates high- from low-KL states on the evaluation episodes (AUROC \res{p1.cartpole.primary.probe_eval_auroc}{.3f}) and on the same episodes with $\sigma{=}0.1$ Gaussian observation noise (\res{p1.cartpole.primary.table2.auroc_noisy}{.3f}). A probe fitted after projecting out the top-50 principal components of $h_t$ reaches \res{p4.cartpole.pc_scrubbed.eval_auroc}{.3f}, and a one-hidden-layer MLP probe \res{p4.cartpole.mlp.eval_auroc}{.3f} (Table~\ref{tab:crosstask} gives all tasks).

\subsection{Temporal Accumulation}

On cartpole, regressing the readout on $C_t$ explains $R^2 = \res{p1.cartpole.primary.variants.C.r2.point}{.2f}$ of its variance, against $R^2 = \res{p1.cartpole.primary.r2_readout_kl.point}{.2f}$ for current KL alone. Because $C_t$ contains the probe's own label, the decisive comparison uses $\Cp$, which excludes the current step: $R^2(\text{readout}, \Cp) = \res{p1.cartpole.primary.variants.C_past.r2.point}{.2f}$, and $\Cp$ explains a fraction \res{p1.cartpole.primary.variants.C_past.partial_r2_beyond_flag.point}{.3f} [\res{p1.cartpole.primary.variants.C_past.partial_r2_beyond_flag.lo}{.3f}, \res{p1.cartpole.primary.variants.C_past.partial_r2_beyond_flag.hi}{.3f}] of the readout variance left unexplained by the current high-KL indicator (partial $R^2$).\confirm{C^past above current KL on every task, Table 2} The readout is better described by recent accumulated difficulty than by the present step's surprise.

Temporal order matters. A ridge regression from $h_t$, fitted on the probe-fit episodes, predicts real-order $C_t$ on the evaluation episodes far better than $C_t$ recomputed with the past flags permuted within each episode and the current flag held fixed ($z = \res{p1.cartpole.primary.ridge_scramble.C.z}{+.1f}$, \res{p1.reacher.primary.ridge_scramble.C.z}{+.1f} and \res{p1.pendulum.primary.ridge_scramble.C.z}{+.1f} on cartpole, reacher and pendulum; Figure~\ref{fig:emergence}d). Removing the direction confirms that the readout depends on it: ablating it ($h_t' = h_t - v\,v^\top h_t$) lowers the readout at $z = \res{p3.cartpole.primary.nulls.null50.ablate_r0.z}{+.1f}$, \res{p3.reacher.primary.nulls.null50.ablate_r0.z}{+.1f} and \res{p3.pendulum.primary.nulls.null50.ablate_r0.z}{+.1f} against the 50-direction null.

\section{\uppercase{Predictive Difficulty Predicts Future Imagination Error}}
\label{sec:external}

To determine whether the discovered history means more than its KL-derived discovery target, we evaluate it against an external quantity defined entirely from imagined and real future trajectories: $\Est_{t,K}$ (Equation~\ref{eq:estate}).

\subsection{Horizon Dependence}

$C_t$ is correlated with $\Est_{t,K}$ at every horizon $K \in \{1,5,10,20\}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:external}a; at $K{=}10$, $r = \res{p2.cartpole.horizons.10.C.point}{.2f}$, \res{p2.reacher.horizons.10.C.point}{.2f} and \res{p2.pendulum.horizons.10.C.point}{.2f} on cartpole, reacher and pendulum).\confirm{sign and horizon trend per task}

\subsection{Incremental Information}

Because $C_t$ is built from KL, and KL may itself predict future error, a standalone correlation is not sufficient. We fit
\begin{equation}
\Est_{t,10} \sim S_t + \KL_t + \Rec_t + \EMA_t + \EMAKL_t,
\label{eq:incr}
\end{equation}
where $S_t$ is $C_t$ or $\Cp$ and $\EMA_t$ and $\EMAKL_t$ are exponential moving averages of $\Rec_t$ and $\KL_t$ whose smoothing constants are chosen on the calibration episodes to best predict $\Est_{t,10}$, giving the smoothing baselines their best chance. We report $\Delta R^2 = R^2_{\text{full}} - R^2_{\text{without } S_t}$ (Table~\ref{tab:external}, Figure~\ref{fig:external}b).

A representation that predicts a quantity need not be the mechanism that produces the capability associated with that quantity. We therefore measure two outcomes of every intervention: the model's \emph{internal} predictive-difficulty readout, and the \emph{external} quality of its imagined predictions, $\Est$.

\section{\uppercase{Interpretation and Scope}}
\label{sec:interpretation}

\subsection{What the Representation Represents}

The evidence identifies \emph{accumulated predictive difficulty}: a discounted memory of recent high-surprise steps, readable from the recurrent state beyond the current step, and informative about how far imagination will depart from reality.

\subsection{What It Does Not Establish}

The representation should not be read as subjective uncertainty, as a universal detector of model failure, or as a mechanism of predictive quality. It is derived from the model's own surprise signal, so it reflects difficulty the model has registered. Its causal role, as tested here, is limited to self-report (Section~\ref{sec:causal}). The probe is linear and trained on a binary label; the MLP and principal-component-scrubbed probes in Table~\ref{tab:crosstask} bound, but do not exhaust, what other readouts could find.

\subsection{A Filtering Interpretation}

Filtering theory explains why a history of surprise should predict future error. For a correctly specified, stationary linear-Gaussian system, the Kalman filter's innovations are white and its error covariance converges to the fixed point of the Riccati recursion independently of the observations \cite{kalman1960filter}; past innovation magnitudes then add nothing. Under misspecification or non-stationarity, innovations become serially correlated and heteroscedastic, the structure adaptive filtering exploits \cite{mehra1970adaptive}, and accumulated evidence of mismatch becomes predictive. In short, persistent mismatch produces state-dependent accumulated evidence.

A scalar Kalman-filter simulation (three regimes $\times$ 10 seeds, with a pre-registered validity criterion for the regime manipulation) reproduces this pattern in a controlled analogue. A $C_t$ analogue built from innovations, evaluated beyond the filter's own instantaneous innovation magnitude and its reported posterior variance, adds $\Delta R^2 = +0.0004 \pm 0.0003$ in the stationary, correctly specified regime, $+0.0046 \pm 0.0018$ under misspecification ($z = +13.3$), and $+0.0825 \pm 0.0142$ under non-stationarity ($z = +257.6$). A world model trained on finite data is never a correctly specified filter, which offers a principled reason for the representation to emerge and to carry information.

\subsection{Cross-Task Interpretation}

The filtering account predicts that the external value of accumulated difficulty should be larger where the model's surprise is more temporally structured; we stated this prediction before computing the diagnostic. The lag-1 autocorrelation of $\KL_t$ on the evaluation episodes is \res{p6.cartpole.acf_kl.1}{.2f}, \res{p6.reacher.acf_kl.1}{.2f} and \res{p6.pendulum.acf_kl.1}{.2f} on cartpole, reacher and pendulum (of the high-KL indicator: \res{p6.cartpole.acf_flag.1}{.2f}, \res{p6.reacher.acf_flag.1}{.2f}, \res{p6.pendulum.acf_flag.1}{.2f}), against incremental effects of \res{p2.cartpole.table3.C_past.delta_r2.point}{+.4f}, \res{p2.reacher.table3.C_past.delta_r2.point}{+.4f} and \res{p2.pendulum.table3.C_past.delta_r2.point}{+.4f} for $\Cp$.\confirm{whether the ordering agrees with the prediction; with three tasks this is descriptive only} With three tasks this comparison is descriptive, and we do not estimate each task's degree of misspecification directly.

\subsection{Scope}

The study uses one RSSM family at one scale, with vector observations, on three dm-control tasks, with data from a uniform-random policy. Pixel observations, larger world models, data from learned policies, and comparisons with methods that train states to be predictive of future quantities are outside its scope.

\section{\uppercase{Discussion}}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What have we learned?} Recurrent world models spontaneously encode predictive difficulty in their hidden states. The representation emerges without supervision, summarises the history of difficulty rather than only its current value, and is readable beyond the current step.

\textbf{Why is it interesting?} The representation is temporally structured and externally meaningful. It predicts a quantity defined entirely outside the signal from which it was discovered, and it does so beyond the instantaneous and smoothed statistics that a designer would construct by hand, across the definitions of the history we tested.

\textbf{What is the deeper lesson?} Internal self-monitoring representations should not automatically be interpreted as the causal mechanisms of the capabilities they report. Had we measured only the readout, steering along $v$ would have appeared to be strong causal evidence for a difficulty mechanism; measuring external quality in the same intervention revealed the dissociation. We recommend that causal claims about self-monitoring representations report both outcomes.

% ===========================================================================
\section{\uppercase{Conclusion}}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A recurrent world model trained with no supervision for predictive difficulty carries, in its deterministic state, a readable and temporally accumulated representation of that difficulty, and this representation predicts future divergence between imagination and reality beyond instantaneous and smoothed error statistics.

Intervention separates its two roles: the readout direction controls the model's internal report of predictive difficulty, and that report persists through the recurrent dynamics, while the external quality of the model's predictions does not move with it.

Learned recurrent systems can develop internal monitors of predictive difficulty that are informative about, but causally separable from, the quality of their own predictions.
